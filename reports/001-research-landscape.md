# Unanswered questions beyond the dark universe

Prepared 8 November 2026. This is a selective literature scouting report, not an exhaustive review or a claim to have identified an untouched field.

## What counts as a Millennium question?

The [Clay Millennium Prize Problems](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/) are seven specific mathematical problems. Wormholes, cosmic origins, and emergent spacetime are not on that list. They are foundational open research questions, without an automatic million-dollar or Nobel award for a proposed answer. A Nobel Prize recognizes an achievement; it does not define a checklist of problems available to solve.

The ambition here is to develop a precise, useful contribution to a foundational question. Complete solutions to several such questions cannot be promised. A successful first outcome might be a reproduced calculation, a new bound under explicit assumptions, an identified degeneracy, or a model that fails a meaningful test.

Einstein's example is valuable because physical principles became equations and quantitative predictions. Observations test predictions within a regime and precision; they do not prove a theory universally. Black-hole theory also developed through contributions from many researchers over the following century.

## Shortlist

| Question | Established starting point | What remains unresolved | A tractable entry point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Can traversable wormholes physically exist?** | Explicit geometries and specialized semiclassical constructions exist. | A physically supported, stable configuration with a credible formation history and applicability to our Universe; observational existence remains unestablished. | Reproduce one construction and audit its matter source, energy restrictions, stability assumptions, and propagation of signals. [W1–W4] |
| **2. Does spacetime emerge from quantum entanglement?** | Holographic models relate geometric quantities to quantum information. | How to obtain realistic cosmological spacetime and a discriminating experimental consequence from microscopic degrees of freedom. | Reproduce a small tensor-network example and distinguish an assumed geometric dictionary from a derived result. [Q1] |
| **3. How is information recovered from an evaporating black hole?** | Entropy calculations including islands reproduce unitary-looking entropy evolution in controlled settings. | A sufficiently complete account of realistic evaporation, operational information recovery, and which assumptions extend beyond tractable models. | Compare the assumptions and accessible observables of two explicit evaporation models. [Q2] |
| **4. Was the hot Big Bang preceded by another physical phase?** | The observed expanding hot Universe motivates early-universe models; inflation is constrained by observations. | Whether a prior phase existed, whether a bounce or other extension is realized, and whether distinctive evidence survives. “Before” may not be defined in every model. | Choose a specific bounce model and test whether its predicted primordial spectrum is distinguishable after nuisance parameters are varied. [C1, C2] |
| **5. What is the global topology of space?** | Local spatial curvature does not uniquely fix global topology; CMB searches constrain particular topologies. | Whether the spatial Universe is multiply connected, and what can be inferred if its topological scale exceeds the observable region. | Generate matched-circle signals on synthetic skies and quantify sensitivity to masking and noise. [C3] |
| **6. What replaces a spacetime singularity?** | Classical singularity results signal limits of classical spacetime descriptions under stated assumptions. | Which quantum description, if any, yields a consistent extension, what “resolution” should mean, and how to test it. | Compare curvature, geodesic, and quantum-evolution criteria in one explicit model. [C2] |
| **7. Does physics prevent usable time machines?** | Some spacetime constructions motivate closed timelike curves; causality restrictions depend on the model. | Whether a general physical protection mechanism survives quantum effects and all relevant assumptions. | Audit a restricted theorem or model instead of asserting a universal conclusion. [W5] |

Sources and reading depth are in the [reading list](../references/reading-list.md). These questions overlap: wormholes connect geometry, quantum fields, information, and causality. None is a blank field in which no work has been done.

## Recommended direction

**Wormhole viability and signal transmission** fits the expressed preference for a speculative object with no established astronomical detection. It offers both foundational mathematics and calculable questions. The objective is not to assume a wormhole exists, but to determine exactly what a proposed model allows and what would invalidate it.

A useful distinction has already emerged from the literature. “Traversable” can mean that an appropriate path exists, while the probability of transmitting a particular wave can still be very small. [A 2026 study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12528) examines this in a magnetic wormhole model and finds strongly probe-dependent behavior. This motivates a study of assumptions and robustness, not a claim that the distinction is our discovery.

**Alternative if spacetime geometry itself is unappealing:** global cosmic topology offers a more observational and computational project; emergent spacetime offers a more quantum-information-centered project. Both still need a precise model and an honest account of what their calculations establish.

## What would constitute progress?

An informative result might be that a proposed signature disappears when a neglected boundary condition changes, or that no region of a specified model meets all the chosen requirements. Such a result can be scientifically useful even when it makes wormholes less plausible.

Do not try to solve all seven questions simultaneously. Keep this map as the backlog, select one paper to reproduce, and let that reproduction expose the next answerable question.
