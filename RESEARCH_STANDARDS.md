# Research standards

Every substantive claim must be labeled as one of: established result under stated assumptions; observational inference; published proposal; our reproduction; our conjecture; or unresolved question.

For each proposed idea, record the question, assumptions, equations, units, prior work, observable or mathematical output, and a result that would reject the idea. A mathematical solution alone does not establish a physically realizable source, stability, formation, or observational existence.

Record source versions and reading depth. An abstract supports triage, not verification of a paper's derivation. Check the full text, errata, critiques, author replies, and later papers before relying on a disputed result. A negative literature search cannot establish novelty.

Keep scripts, parameters, units, numerical convergence checks, data provenance, and figure-generation instructions with each calculation. Distinguish illustrative geometry from simulated dynamics and observed images. A visualization is explanatory, not evidence.

AI assistance is used for literature triage, drafting, algebra, code, and criticism. Human authors must verify citations, derivations, numerical behavior, originality, and interpretation. Do not fabricate data or present generated text as peer review. Record who checked each result before submission.

For public data, retain original acknowledgments and licenses. Link to papers rather than redistributing their figures or full text without checking permissions. Decide authorship, code/content licensing, and repository visibility before publication.
