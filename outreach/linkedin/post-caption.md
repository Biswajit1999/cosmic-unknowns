Would a wormhole signal be unique?

In my latest computational study, I ask a measurement question: can scalar-wave transmission distinguish an Ellis wormhole model from a competing wave barrier?

I ran 150,000 synthetic experiments across 25 count budgets per frequency band, with 24 channels and explicit uncertainty estimates. Under ideal counting assumptions, broad-band rejection of the fitted comparator reaches 92.1% at 3,162 incident quanta per channel (pointwise 95% Wilson interval: 90.26–93.62%).

The next result changes the interpretation. If independent false-positive and false-negative errors are allowed in each channel, I can construct a competing model that matches all 24 broad-band recorded probabilities with an error allowance of about 3.0943%. For this matched pair, collecting more counts cannot distinguish the models through these measurements alone.

This is a result about model identification under stated assumptions. It is not an astronomical detection or proof that physical wormholes exist. The error allowance is a constructive numerical witness, not a measured detector specification or a certified minimum. Specialist review remains pending.

The implication for future instruments is practical: sensitivity and detector calibration must be studied together.

My report, derivations, code and synthetic data are public:
https://github.com/Biswajit1999/cosmic-unknowns

I welcome technical feedback, particularly on realistic detector-response constraints and independent numerical replication.
